# U.S. Labor Market & H-1B Sponsorship Analysis

## Executive Summary
In response to the 2025 H-1B fee increase, this team project (three of us, for DAT-5486 Data Management & SQL at Hult) analyzes **561,036 Labor Condition Applications (LCAs) from FY2022 to FY2024**, the forms employers file to hire someone on an H-1B, to find the most realistic job paths for international STEM graduates. By adjusting nominal wages (the salary on paper) for cost of living (COL) with Bureau of Economic Analysis data, the study challenges the "Tech Hub" bias: lower cost states can offer more real purchasing power.

## Tools Used
* **Language:** SQL (Azure MySQL) for aggregating and filtering the data
* **Analysis:** Cost of living (COL) indexing, wage gap analysis
* **Focus:** Three target roles for business analytics graduates (IT Project Managers, BI Analysts, Financial & Investment Analysts)

## Key Findings
* **The "Real Wage" Shift:** California pays BI analysts more on paper ($132K vs $106K in Arkansas), but once you adjust for cost of living, **Arkansas** comes out ahead (**$120K** vs **$116K** in California). We recommended lower cost growth states, namely **Texas**, **Georgia** and **North Carolina**.
* **Target Roles:** The three target roles made up **over 7%** of all FY2024 LCA filings, at an average nominal wage of **$108,072**.
* **Strategic Employers:** Identified a specific tier of employers (e.g., Amazon, Infosys) that consistently sponsor F-1 STEM OPT graduates (international students working on their student visa after a U.S. STEM degree), and recommended a recruiting strategy aimed at these firms to keep immigration risk low.
